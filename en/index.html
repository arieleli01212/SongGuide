Step 1 of 72 minutes

Choose the person and the moment

This decision shapes everything that follows. Do not skip it.

The most common mistake is starting with “I want to make a song” and only then deciding who it is for. That creates something polished that touches nobody.

Start with the person. Pick one specific person — not “my family” or “the team at work.”

Three questions before you continue

  1. Who? One person. Use their first name.
  2. When will they hear it? A birthday, wedding, graduation, goodbye, or an ordinary Tuesday.
  3. What should they feel? Joy? Longing? Seen? Choose one feeling.
Tip: “An ordinary Tuesday” works beautifully. A song for no occasion is often more surprising than a birthday song.

Why this matters

✕ No clear choice“An emotional birthday song for someone special” sounds like it could be for anyone.
✓ A real choice“A song for Mom's 60th that makes her feel we noticed everything she gave up” gives the song somewhere real to go.

Step 2 of 75 minutes

Answer the questionnaire

Eight questions. In the next step, your answers become a ready-to-copy prompt automatically.

✍️ Write here — do not just read

Your answers stay in your browser. In step 3 they are assembled into a prompt, so you will not have to copy each one manually.

The difference between a moving song and generic AI output is the specificity of what you give it. The tool can write. It knows nothing about your person.

Short answers are fine. Make them specific.

Question 1

A nickname usually feels more personal than a formal name.
Question 2

What they play alone in the car — not what they say they like.

Step 4 of 72 minutes

Make every name pronounceable

A beautiful song falls apart the moment it says their name wrong.

Suno sometimes guesses the pronunciation of names, places, abbreviations, and words borrowed from another language. Do not wait until the final version to discover the mistake.

Use a pronunciation pass

  1. Highlight every name, place, and unusual word in the lyrics.
  2. Read each one out loud exactly as it is written.
  3. If it is ambiguous, replace it with a phonetic spelling or split it into syllables.
  4. Generate a short test before spending time on the full song.
✕ AmbiguousSiobhan may be guessed incorrectly.
✓ Pronunciation cueShi-vawn gives the model a clearer signal.
Writing a song in Hebrew? Use the free Dicta Nakdan to add niqqud, especially to names. It is more reliable for Hebrew vocalization than asking a general-purpose language model.

Step 5 of 71 minute

Choose a musical style

Choose for their taste, not yours.

Suno has a style field separate from the lyrics. Describe what the person really listens to.

The rule

Keep the production cleaner than you think. Dense arrangements bury the words, and the words are the gift.

Style example
intimate acoustic pop ballad, warm vocal, slow tempo, sparse arrangement, clear lyrics, emotional but restrained
  • Intimate and warm help the song feel close.
  • Slow tempo keeps the words understandable.
  • Sparse arrangement leaves room for the story.
Male or female voice? Think about who is “speaking” in the song — you. A voice closer to your own often feels more personal.

Step 6 of 72 minutes

Generate, judge, and repair

The first version is almost never the final one.

  1. Open Suno and choose Custom.
  2. Paste the checked lyrics into the lyrics field.
  3. Paste your style into the style field.
  4. Generate — you will receive two versions.

Judge with three questions

  • Can you hear every word? If not, the style is too busy or too fast.
  • Are the names pronounced correctly? If not, return to step 4.
  • Does the chorus stick? If not, it may be too long. Shorten it.
Repair instead of restarting: If one line is wrong, use Song Editor → Replace Section. Regenerate only that section and keep what already works.
Expect three or four versions. That is normal. Almost nobody gets the final song on the first try.

Step 7 of 71 minute

Deliver the song

The easiest part to overlook may be the most important.

You made something meaningful. Do not bury it between two ordinary messages.

Three rules

  • Be there. Play it in person or on a video call. You want to see the moment.
  • Do not explain first. “I made something for you — listen.” That is enough.
  • Stay quiet while it plays. Resist the urge to apologize for the production.
If they ask “Is this AI?” Say yes without apologizing. The tool performed it. The words, details, and thought are yours.
